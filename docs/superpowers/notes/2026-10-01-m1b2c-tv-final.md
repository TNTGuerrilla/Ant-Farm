# M1b-2c: the final TV test (2026-10-01)

TCL 65QM6K (Mali-G52, GLES 3.2), `profile` build, logs streamed to files on the PC (`build/tv-m1b2c-*.log`). The target (spec 1, item 4): in the 3D view, after the first 10 s, an average of at least 58 fps and at most 1% of frames over 25 ms, for both the starter colony and the 1,000-ant colony.

In the starter screensaver run the nest and overhead views held 57 to 61 fps; in the 1,000-ant runs the overhead held 60 fps throughout. No crash or GL error in any run.

**Both runs pass on the final build (a60a3c0).**

## How the 1,000-ant run got there

A layer-skip profile (`PreviewActivity --es layers ...`, five 45 s runs on 3961bf7) showed the frame was over budget by about 5 ms in total, not because of one layer: skipping the ground, or the ants, alone gave 60 fps. The TV transforms about 30,000 vertices per millisecond, and the frame submitted about 620,000 (ground 352k flat-shaded non-indexed, rocks 140k, ants about 110k, grass and shadows about 13k each).

The cuts, in order:
1. **Ant culling and LOD** (471cdd9): ants outside the view or beyond 1.2 m are not drawn; the detailed model only for the nearest ants; shadows only nearby.
2. **Cheaper ant shader** (0765be8): gait trigonometry and the ant's frame computed once per ant on the CPU; at most 80 detailed ants within 25 cm; shadows within 40 cm.
3. **Outer-ring ground** (a60a3c0): the inner 3 by 3 chunks keep 48 cells (10.4 mm facets); the outer ring uses 14 cells inside a stitched border on the fine grid, so the levels meet exactly. Ground went from about 352k to 157k vertices.
4. **Indexed rocks** (a60a3c0): rocks are smooth-shaded, so they share vertices (about 23k unique, 142k indices).

The final frame submits about 400k vertices with the ant crowd in view.

## Bugs found during the test and fixed

- **Ants stuck on rock faces** (1508f46, 340c928): an empty forager near a plant re-faced the plant flatly every tick and parked at a rock's silhouette, its heading flipping each tick (the "spinning" clumps). Ants now detour to a random side when they stop making progress toward a plant or the entrance in sight, with each repeated detour longer and wider. Over 25,000 ticks of seed 1, ants stuck 20 s or more went from 17 to 0. Vector homing has no detour (it re-aims once per run and cannot park; a detour there broke the Gruter test).
- **Ants floating over large rocks** (3961bf7): rocks of 60 mm and more were drawn 1 to 9 mm inside their true surface. Rocks now use subdivision 3 (30 mm and more) and 4 (80 mm and more), with vertices nudged out by half the chord sag; facets stay within about 0.2 mm of the surface.
